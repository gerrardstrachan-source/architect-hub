# H2 — Prospective Future-Learning / Structural-Transfer Experiment

## Status

**Prospective design only. No H2 data exist. H1 remains frozen and closed.**

## Scientific purpose

H2 is designed to challenge the surviving ACT-R account after H1 established that its state-specific behavior is adequate to explain the H1 result.

H2 must not begin by assuming an understanding mechanism. Its purpose is to test whether a pattern of future learning can be adequately explained by the surviving architectural account.

## Central question

> After systems acquire the same initial task history and comparable immediate performance, does structurally organized future learning produce a characteristic multi-condition learning trajectory that the surviving state-specific ACT-R account cannot adequately explain?

## Core design

**TRAINING → latent-structure exposure → comparable immediate performance → new task family → differential future-learning trajectory**

The critical evidence is not a single transfer success/failure. The target is a pre-specified pattern across multiple future conditions.

## Conditions

### A — Compatible extension

The underlying relation remains compatible with prior experience while surface particulars change.

### B — Structural recombination

Previously encountered elements appear in a novel combination or relation.

### C — Surface transformation

The relevant relational structure remains constant while the superficial representation changes.

### D — Structural conflict

A previously useful response organization becomes misleading under a changed relational structure.

## Primary measures

- trials-to-criterion
- trial-by-trial accuracy
- error-transition pattern
- action/strategy sequence
- transfer performance
- interference cost
- retention after delay/block separation where applicable
- response to increased structural complexity

## Pre-registration requirements

Before execution, H2 must specify:

1. exact training stimuli/states and feedback schedule;
2. exact representation available to the ACT-R model;
3. exact ACT-R parameters and learning mechanisms;
4. exact future-task sequences for A–D;
5. the rival model or structural account, if retained for comparison;
6. quantitative predictions for each condition;
7. the criterion for calling the future-learning signature a model failure rather than ordinary stochastic variation;
8. the replication/separation rule for distinguishing a single anomaly from a systematic pattern;
9. all primary and secondary outcome measures;
10. the stopping rule and exclusions, specified before data collection.

## Main methodological constraint

H2 must not define success as “ACT-R fails and a new understanding mechanism succeeds.”

The admissible sequence is:

**phenomenon → competing predictions → pre-specified observations → model comparison → surviving-model adequacy test → only then residual assessment**

## Residual criterion

A candidate residual is not established merely because a rival model outperforms ACT-R in one condition.

A stronger residual claim requires a reproducible, pre-specified, multi-condition phenomenon for which:

- the surviving ACT-R account makes a substantive prediction;
- the prediction is quantitatively specific enough to be tested;
- the implementation is verified;
- the observed learning trajectory is reliably outside that account's adequate explanatory range;
- the discrepancy is not attributable to an implementation defect, parameter misspecification, stochastic sampling alone, or an omitted mechanism already belonging to the established ACT-R account;
- the pattern persists under an appropriate replication or independent trajectory test.

## H1 continuity

H1 is not to be modified or rerun merely to create H2 evidence.

The H1 record remains:

- H1 implementation: closed
- H1 runtime: verified
- H1 model discrimination: passed
- H1 ACT-R explanatory adequacy: adequate for H1
- architectural residual: not established

## Design principle

> A model-discriminating phenomenon is not an architectural residual merely because the models disagree; the residual begins only when the surviving account cannot adequately explain the observed phenomenon.
